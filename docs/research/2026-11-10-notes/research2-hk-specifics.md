### 1.6 Disclosure of Interests (SFO Part XV)
- SFC "Outline of Part XV", revised 24 May 2024: https://www.sfc.hk/-/media/EN/assets/components/codes/files-current/web/outline-of-part-xv-of-the-securities-and-futures-ordinance-cap571-disclosure-of-interests/Part-XV-Outline-24-May-2024-English.pdf. I downloaded the PDF and read the text. **page**
- Substantial shareholders: interested in "5% or more" of any class of voting shares of a listed corporation (para 1.1). A new duty arises when the interest crosses a whole percentage level above 5% (e.g. 6.8% to 7.1%); a short position triggers at 1% (paras 2.5, 2.6). **page**
- Directors and chief executives: must disclose interests and short positions "in any shares" of the listed corporation or associated corporations, plus debentures (para 1.1(ii) and section 3). No percentage threshold. **page**
- Deadline: para 2.7.2 says 10 business days for an "Initial Notification" and "3 business days" for the other relevant events. **page**
- Definition (para 4.1.3): "business day" means a day other than a Saturday, a public holiday, and a day on which a black rainstorm warning or a gale warning is in force (text continues; I read only the opening). Note: the 2003-era summaries say Saturdays count; the 2024 outline excludes Saturday. **page**
- HKEX search site: "Disclosure of Interests Online System" https://sdinotice.hkex.com.hk/ (filing) and the search pages at https://di.hkex.com.hk/di/ (live today: NSAllFormList.aspx). Hub page: https://www2.hkexnews.hk/shareholding-disclosures/disclosure-of-interests?sc_lang=en. **page**

## 2. Academic evidence specific to Hong Kong
---------------------------------------------------------------------
For every item I opened the abstract page. Sample periods and effect sizes appear only where the abstract states them; otherwise "not in abstract".

1. **Southbound flows and returns.** Ding, Song, Bian, Zhang (2025), Pacific-Basin Finance Journal 93. Abstract: southbound flows "can significantly predict short-term returns on Hong Kong stocks"; after the Fama-French five-factor adjustment, a weekly rebalanced long-short portfolio gets "an annualized return of up to 25.84 %". Net of costs: not stated. Sample period: not in abstract (full text is behind a paywall). Link: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/pacfin/v93y2025ics0927538x25002240.html. **page**
2. **Short-sale constraints.** Chang, Cheng, Yu (2007), Journal of Finance 62(5). Abstract: stocks added to the HK designated short-sale list show effects consistent with prior overvaluation, stronger "for ... wider dispersion of investor opinions"; "higher volatility and less positive skewness" when short sales are allowed. No return-prediction effect size, no costs, sample period not in abstract. Link: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v62y2007i5p2097-2121.html. **page**. (This is about list additions, not daily short-selling turnover as a signal.) I found no HK study on short-selling turnover or SFC short-position data as a return predictor. **unconfirmed**
3. **Directors' dealings.** Firth, Leung, Rui, "Insider trading in Hong Kong: tests of stock returns and trading frequency", Review of Pacific Basin Financial Markets and Policies 14(3), 2011. Abstract: sample is 1993 to 1999. "insider sales are more informative than purchases". "On average, insiders earn HK$91,297 per trade, while outsiders who mimic insiders' transactions earn minimal returns." Net of costs: not stated. Link: https://scholars.ln.edu.hk/en/publications/insider-trading-in-hong-kong-tests-of-stock-returns-and-trading-f/. **page**. I found no later HK directors' dealings study (search returned only the 2010 "Double signals or single signal?" paper on insiders around buybacks; **snippet only**).
4. **Share buybacks.** Firth and Yeung (2005), Journal of Emerging Market Finance 4(3). Abstract: firms starting buybacks "have surplus cash and are undervalued"; market-adjusted returns around the first buyback depend on prior abnormal returns and shares bought. Sample and sizes not in abstract. Link: https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:emffin:v:4:y:2005:i:3:p:207-225. **page**. Two Erasmus theses reporting short-term gains and zero long-run abnormal returns (2011 to 2018 and 2004 to 2012 samples): **snippet only**.
5. **Momentum, value, size (Asia Pacific, includes HK).** Fama and French (2012), JFE 105(3). Abstract: in four regions including Asia Pacific "there are value premiums ... that, except for Japan, decrease with size"; "Except for Japan, there is return momentum everywhere", and it also falls from small to big stocks. No HK-only numbers, no costs. Sample period not in abstract. Link: https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:105:y:2012:i:3:p:457-472. **page**
6. **Momentum across countries.** Chui, Titman, Wei (2010), Journal of Finance 65(1). Abstract: momentum profits rise with a country's individualism score and fall with firm size and volatility. It names no HK figures. East-Asia weakness and the 41-country, 1980 to 2003 sample come from a secondary summary. Link: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v65y2010i1p361-392.html. **page** (abstract); sample/East Asia remark **snippet only**.
7. **Short-term reversal.** Tang and Zhang (2014), Applied Economics 46(12). Abstract: weekly data; reversal is "pervasive within past winner stocks only"; contrarian profits "significantly positive" especially for small and illiquid firms; but "arbitrage trading costs would largely overwhelm the available profits in most cases". Sample period not in abstract. Link: https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/applec/v46y2014i12p1335-1349.html. **page**
8. **52-week high, low volatility, value in HK-only studies:** I found none. The search returned only the US George-Hwang paper (snippet; HK coverage unverified). **unconfirmed**
9. **US overnight to HK open.** No academic paper testing overnight US returns against the HK open was found. A related 2025 Pacific-Basin Finance Journal paper on US-to-China volatility spillovers with Hong Kong as intermediary exists (https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/pacfin/v90y2025ics0927538x25000071.html, **snippet only**, abstract not opened).
10. **ADR vs HK-listed gaps.** Study: "The Law of One Price and Arbitrage on China's Dual-Listings in Hong Kong and New York", International Journal of Business and Finance 9(2), 2012. Abstract: with Chinese ADRs and their HK shares, a simple strategy shows "arbitrage profits are still available with monthly return ranging from 0.5 per cent to 3.8 per cent after considering transaction costs and non-overlap trading time issues". Sample period and which names: not in abstract; Alibaba, JD, Baidu not named. Link: https://e-journal.uum.edu.my/index.php/ijbf/article/view/8453. **page**. (2012 paper: predates the 2019/2020 HK secondary listings of Alibaba, JD, Baidu.)
11. **A-H premium.** Liu and Tang (arXiv 2602.14754v3, May 2026), 67 A-H firms, monthly, 2011 to 2019: Shanghai-HK Connect "associated with an average 18.4% increase in the A-H price premium". Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14754. **page**. Pan and Chi (2021), Emerging Markets Finance and Trade 57(7): 56 firms, Nov 2011 to Nov 2017, Connect "significantly reduces the A-H share premium"; link https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:mes:emfitr:v:57:y:2021:i:7:p:1912-1928. **page**. The two disagree on direction. Neither tests the premium as a return signal. No paper on the Hang Seng Stock Connect China AH Premium Index as a signal was found. **unconfirmed**
12. **Profit warnings.** Yin, Mazouz, Benamraoui, Saadouni, Review of Quantitative Finance and Accounting (2018), HK-listed firms. Abstract: standard event study shows reversals after positive and negative warnings; with time-varying betas, event-induced variance and conditional heteroskedasticity, negative-warning patterns "consistent with" market efficiency and positive-warning drift is small enough that "minor transaction costs would eliminate the profitability". Sample period not in abstract. Link: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11156-017-0623-3. **page**
13. **Post-earnings drift in HK.** Only snippets: a PolyU thesis (SEHK firms 1988 to 1996, drift under 60 days for positive surprises), two 2021 conference papers on Stock Connect with opposite signs, and a Global Network note that HK drift fell near zero after IFRS adoption. **snippet only**. No abstract page opened.
14. **Retail investors.** HKEX Cash Market Transaction Survey 2020 (latest on the HKEX surveys page, "Updated 22 Apr 2022"): https://www.HKEX.com.hk/-/media/HKEX-Market/News/Research-Reports/HKEX-Surveys/Cash-Market-Transaction-Survey-2020/CMTS2020_e.pdf. Local retail investors 10.2% of turnover; overseas retail 5.3%; retail total about 15% (from the report text; 2019 local figure 13.6% is **snippet only**). Holding-period data: none found in HKEX surveys; the IFEC "Retail Investor Study 2023" has a "Duration of Holding a Stock" section (https://www.ifec.org.hk/web/common/pdf/about-ifec/retail-investor-study-2023.pdf, **snippet only**; figures not read). No SFC retail survey found.
